2) Market map — "The opportunity for <Company>":
   - subtitle: the customer need that defines this market, as one plain sentence.
   - axes: two real buying criteria in this vertical, each a SCALE from a fixed list. Set each axis's kind to
     one of these (x and y must differ); low_label is the low end (left on x, bottom on y), high_label the
     high end (right / top):
       customer_size         small / single-site  ->  large / multi-entity / enterprise
       customer_complexity   simple programs  ->  complex programs (many retailers, entities, compliance)
       product_scope         point tool / ops only  ->  full suite (incl. financials, WMS...)
       industry_depth        generic / horizontal  ->  built for this industry
       deployment_modernity  desktop / on-prem / legacy  ->  cloud-native
       segment_focus         other segments  ->  the target's segment
     The title and half-labels put that scale in this vertical's words. A reader must be able to say which
     end is "more": never an axis of buyer types or channels. The target sits in the high/high (top-right)
     cell AND the evidence must support that.
   - competitors: place every competitor you were given (choose 5-8; drop the weakest if more) into a cell.
     Max 3 logos per cell including the target; use at least 3 of the 4 cells.
     - For EVERY competitor cite x_evidence_ids and y_evidence_ids: at least one quote each that states where
       it sits on that axis. Prefer quotes from the competitor's own site or a neutral directory (Capterra,
       G2, GetApp, Software Advice, SoftwareConnect, TrustRadius) over a rival's page about it; no single
       website should supply more than 40% of the quotes on this slide.
     - Not mentioned is not evidence. Never place a company, or write a rationale, from what a page does not
       say ("no financials cited", "lists no warehouse module"). The rationale states what the quotes say.
     - The strongest direct rival goes in or next to the target's cell. The target alone in its cell tells
       the founder they have no competition, and they know that is false.
     - If the thesis or subtitle leads with a customer segment, at least one competitor on the map must serve
       that segment; otherwise don't lead with it.
     - earlier_placements (where earlier decks put a competitor, with their quotes) are the market's memory:
       a placement that contradicts them needs a quote that supports the change.
   - our_take: a bold headline that starts with the company name and states what it does that the others
     don't, plus two short points that explain why each GROUP of competitors falls short (name groups like
     "Horizontal ERPs" or "Single-shop tools", never individual companies). Cite point_evidence_ids: one list
     per point, each with at least one quote showing that group really lacks what the point says.
